LETTER FROM THE AUTHOR TO READERS

In a few words, I'll tell you the story of the story I wrote, as if it were a film in which I jumped into the role of one of the characters, Michele, the codebreaker.

You might say, "But you, physically, perhaps, resemble Gretel." That's not what I was interested in. The deliberate coincidence is in his character; Michele interprets life with an inevitable sense of humor: after struggling with all kinds of problems and their consequences, he finally finds it more reasonable to take them lightly, otherwise you die. And when even laughing about it becomes unbearable, Poetry, with its generous intelligence, comes to the rescue, a bridge to the remission of evil. If a story must have meaning and a message, I would like to offer this: from darkness to light.

Thus, between irony and Poetry, this is a film in which the most vile greed reigns supreme, or at least tries to. And it all originates in Japan, in the time of the samurais and the demons of the sixteenth century, when heads were cut off and hara-kiri were committed.

The protagonist is an ancient dagger with a precious hilt that sets the whole story in motion, of which, however, in reality we have no knowledge: but it is a symbol and a metaphor, a deferred message. It speaks to us of the constants in the chaos of history, of the mysterious solemnity of certain places that become destiny, of characters who, in their earthly exile, trust in the redemption. It speaks of interior landscapes fixed in a stubborn solitude. It reflects the hues of nature in chiaroscuro.

1

And yet, if you look at it from another perspective, that dagger emits the soothing sweetness of its rainbow reflections, the optimism of a golden glow, and has the magical power to change the course of the lives of the Italian, Japanese, and Irish characters.

Since it's a thriller, there's a crime in the story, and I'll tell you about it at length at the beginning. And there's death, but I'll tell you that at the end. Be patient.
